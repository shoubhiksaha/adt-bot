# Run ADT Bot free on Oracle, offload speech

As of late October 2026, only one free cloud server can run the whole ADT Bot stack 24/7: **Oracle Cloud's Always Free Ampere A1 VM**. The bad news is that Oracle **halved it to 2 Arm cores and 12 GB of RAM in June 2026**. The 12 GB still holds the Node server, Chromium and the speech models. Two cores are too slow for Whisper to feel conversational. So the best ₹0 setup splits the work. The Node server, sync, routines and Chromium (and Kokoro, if you want it) run on an A1 VM in Oracle's **Mumbai or Hyderabad** region. Speech-to-text goes to **Groq's free Whisper API**, which plugs into ADT Bot's existing "Cloud Whisper" setting with no code changes. Text-to-speech uses Kokoro on the box or **Azure's free F0 tier / Google Cloud TTS**, both already built into ADT Bot. Every other free offer is one of three things: a tiny VM (GCP's US-only e2-micro, Oracle's 1 GB AMD micro, Azure's 12-month 1 GiB VMs), a container that sleeps or has no disk (Render, Northflank, Hugging Face), or credit that runs out in 1–6 months (AWS, GCP, Linode, Vultr). Free compute shrank sharply in 2025–2026: Oracle's halving, AWS replacing its 12-month free tier, and Hugging Face, Koyeb, Zeabur and Glitch dropping free container hosting. Free hosted speech stayed generous over the same period. If you already own an old 4-core PC or laptop, a home server behind Tailscale is the fastest option for speech and costs only electricity. A Raspberry Pi 5 no longer is, because its price roughly doubled in the 2026 RAM shortage.

## Oracle's free Arm allowance halved to 2 cores and 12 GB in June 2026

Oracle's Always Free tier now gives **1,500 OCPU-hours and 9,000 GB-hours a month of Ampere A1**, which Oracle equates to **2 OCPUs and 12 GB**. You can use it as one VM or as two 1-OCPU VMs. It also includes two AMD micro VMs (1/8 OCPU, 1 GB each), **200 GB of block storage** and **10 TB a month of outbound transfer** ([Oracle Always Free Resources](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier_topic-Always_Free_Resources.htm)). Signup adds a $300, 30-day trial. Always Free resources keep running after the trial ends ([OCI Free Tier overview](https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier.htm)). Until mid-2026 the A1 allowance was twice this size. Oracle cut it from 4 OCPU / 24 GB effective about **15 June 2026**. It changed the docs quietly, with "no blog post, customer email, or public announcement" ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/07/oracle-cloud-free-tier-limits/)). An Oracle email quoted on Hacker News set **18 August 2026** as the deadline, after which over-limit instances "will be automatically terminated" ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49183750)). Pay-as-you-go (PAYG) accounts are disputed: support chats and blogs disagree on whether they still get 4/24 free, and Oracle has not clarified ([terminalbytes](https://terminalbytes.com/oracle-cloud-free-tier-changes-2026/)).

For an Indian user the regions are good and getting in is the hard part. Oracle has **India West (Mumbai, `ap-mumbai-1`) and India South (Hyderabad, `ap-hyderabad-1`)**. Each has **only one availability domain** ([OCI Regions](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/General/Concepts/regions.htm)). That matters because Oracle's standard fix for the common **"Out of host capacity"** error is to try another availability domain, wait, or upgrade to a paid account. Oracle adds that it "doesn't charge for Always Free resources after upgrading, only for usage above the limits" ([Oracle Always Free Resources](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier_topic-Always_Free_Resources.htm)). In India the other-availability-domain option doesn't exist. Your options are retrying (community retry scripts exist), switching fault domains, or the PAYG upgrade. That upgrade reportedly places a **temporary ~$100 card verification charge** ([fullmetalbrackets](https://fullmetalbrackets.com/blog/oci-free-tier-breakdown)). Always Free compute works only in the **home region you pick at signup**, so choose Mumbai or Hyderabad deliberately ([OCI Free Tier overview](https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier.htm)). No recent India capacity reports were found. The only Hyderabad report is about three years old and describes no A1 capacity ([Oracle Customer Connect](https://community.oracle.com/customerconnect/discussion/713991/unable-to-create-instance-vm-standard-a1-flex-out-of-host-capacity)). Signup needs a mobile number and a credit or debit card ([OCI Free Tier overview](https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier.htm)). In February 2026 an Indian user reported their card being rejected repeatedly ([GitHub gist comments](https://gist.github.com/rssnyder/51e3cfedd730e7dd5f4a816143b25dbd?permalink_comment_id=5546553)), and no source confirms whether RuPay or UPI work.

**Idle reclamation shapes how you size the VM.** Oracle can reclaim an Always Free instance if, over 7 days, its 95th-percentile CPU, its network use, *and* (on A1) its memory use all stay **below 20%** ([Oracle Always Free Resources](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier_topic-Always_Free_Resources.htm)). ADT Bot's Node server uses roughly 0.5 GB and Chromium about 1 GB. That is only about 12% of a 12 GB VM, so a Node-plus-Chromium-only box could look idle. There are three ways around this. You can keep the speaches container (Kokoro and a small Whisper) resident, which pushes memory past the 2.4 GB threshold. You can create a 1 OCPU / 6 GB instance instead, where 1.5 GB is 25%. Or you can upgrade to PAYG; community reports say idle PAYG instances were not reclaimed ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49183750)). Docker and 24/7 processes are otherwise unrestricted, since these are ordinary VMs. The repo's `deploy/docker-compose.yml` already targets this setup: it names Oracle's free Arm VM, keeps the port closed in favour of Tailscale, and defaults speech to `faster-whisper-base` plus Kokoro ONNX.

## Two Arm cores can't keep Whisper conversational, but free APIs can

Local testing on 2026-10-22 shows how close to the limit CPU speech already is. ADT Bot's new speech relay and a speaches CPU container ran on an M1 MacBook Air in a Colima VM with 4 vCPUs (arm64). **Whisper base transcribed a 3.3 s clip in about 3.5 s once loaded**, roughly real time; the first request took **9.5 s** while the model loaded. **Kokoro produced 4.8 s of speech in about 2.1 s warm**, about twice real-time speed. Switching speaches to int8 (`WHISPER__COMPUTE_TYPE=int8`) **did not make Whisper base faster** on that busy Mac: it took 4.0–5.0 s for the same 3.3 s clip. Oracle's free allowance is two Neoverse-N1 cores. That is half the core count, and per core it is roughly Raspberry Pi 5 class rather than M1 class. Published Pi 5 numbers set the expectation: stock Kokoro ONNX runs at a real-time factor of **0.98–1.27** on all four Pi cores ([kokoro-pi](https://github.com/zreecespieces/kokoro-pi)). whisper.cpp base only reaches real time with four threads ([promptquorum](https://www.promptquorum.com/power-local-llm/local-whisper-stt-comparison-2026)). Nobody has measured faster-whisper or Kokoro on a 2-OCPU A1, so treat this as an estimate. Still, Whisper base on Oracle will very likely be noticeably slower than real time. Before relying on Oracle for voice, the safer default is to send speech-to-text to **Groq's free Whisper API**, or else drop the local model to `faster-whisper-tiny` via `ADT_SPEECH_STT_MODEL`. Kokoro on the box is usable for patient listeners, and a free cloud voice is faster.

**Groq is the zero-code choice for speech-to-text.** Its endpoint `https://api.groq.com/openai/v1/audio/transcriptions` is OpenAI-compatible and serves `whisper-large-v3-turbo` and `whisper-large-v3` ([Groq STT docs](https://console.groq.com/docs/speech-to-text)). ADT Bot's Cloud Whisper setting accepts any such endpoint, so the setup is a base URL, a key and a model name. The limits table shows **20 requests/minute, 2,000/day, 7,200 audio-seconds/hour and 28,800 audio-seconds/day** for Whisper ([Groq rate limits](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits)). An independent write-up checked in July 2026 confirms these as the Free plan figures ([Spokenly](https://spokenly.app/blog/free-speech-to-text-apis)). Groq's own page labels them "Developer plan base", and your account's Limits page is authoritative. Each request is billed as **at least 10 s** of audio, so short voice turns use up the audio budget faster than their real length. Even so, the **2,000 requests/day cap binds first**, which is far beyond one person's use. Groq keeps no data by default, and its transcription endpoint qualifies for a **Zero Data Retention** toggle ([Groq: Your Data](https://console.groq.com/docs/your-data)). The research did not confirm whether a card is needed. If Groq ever tightens the free plan, OpenAI's `gpt-4o-mini-transcribe` at **$0.003/min** is a drop-in paid fallback: about $0.36 a month at 120 minutes of speech ([OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)).

**For text-to-speech, use providers ADT Bot already has.** Azure's F0 tier gives **0.5 million neural characters a month** (about 10 hours of speech) plus 5 hours of real-time speech-to-text ([Azure Speech pricing](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/pricing/details/cognitive-services/speech-services/)). It is limited to 20 TTS transactions per minute ([Azure quotas](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/speech-services-quotas-and-limits)). It has en-IN, hi-IN and bn-IN voices ([Azure language support](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/language-support?tabs=tts)) and does not persist TTS data ([Azure data privacy](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-foundry/responsible-ai/speech-service/speech-to-text/data-privacy-security)). Google Cloud TTS is even larger: **1M Chirp 3 HD characters and 4M WaveNet/Standard characters free every month**, though "you must enable billing" ([GCP TTS pricing](https://cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/pricing)). Chirp 3 HD covers en-IN, hi-IN and bn-IN ([Chirp 3 HD](https://cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/chirp3-hd)). If you want no card at all, Deepgram gives **$200 of credit that needs no card and doesn't expire** ([Deepgram pricing](https://deepgram.com/pricing)). That pays for years of Aura-2 at hobby volume, but Aura-2 has **no Hindi or Indian-English voice** ([Deepgram TTS models](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/tts-models)). Cartesia's free 20K characters a month (about 24 minutes) does cover Hindi and Bengali ([Cartesia pricing](https://cartesia.ai/pricing)).

Cloudflare has the strongest privacy terms of the group. It contractually commits to "no training" and zero data retention ([Cloudflare data usage](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/data-usage/)), and its free 10,000 neurons/day covers both speech-to-text and text-to-speech ([Cloudflare pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/)). It does not expose OpenAI-style audio routes, though ([Cloudflare OpenAI compatibility](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/configuration/open-ai-compatibility/)), so using it means a small code addition. For Hindi-English code-mixing or Bengali, Sarvam's Saaras v4 has a dedicated codemix mode ([Sarvam Saaras](https://docs.sarvam.ai/api/getting-started/models/saaras)), but its speech-to-text API is not OpenAI-shaped. Avoid Gemini's free tier for a private voice app: Google uses free-tier input to improve products, and "human reviewers may read, annotate, and process your API input and output" ([Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)). If you want self-hosted speech in the cloud anyway, Modal's Starter plan gives **$30 of credit a month** ([Modal pricing](https://modal.com/pricing)). At its published rates, a scale-to-zero container with 2 physical cores and 4 GiB costs about $0.13 an hour, so 100 voice turns a day comes to roughly **$7 a month**. Keeping it warm 24/7 would cost $57–91. Idle warm containers are billed, and each cold start re-loads the models ([Modal cold-start guide](https://modal.com/docs/guide/cold-start)). That load cost the 9.5 s first request in the local test.

## Every other cloud is a trial, a toy, or a container that sleeps

The other hyperscalers have no permanent VM that fits even Node plus Chromium in India. Google's Always Free tier is **one e2-micro in us-west1, us-central1 or us-east1 only**, with 30 GB of disk and **1 GB a month of egress**. Its $300 credit lasts 90 days ([Google Cloud Free Program](https://docs.cloud.google.com/free/docs/free-cloud-features)). Cloud Run's free allowance can't keep an instance up all month: one always-on vCPU works out to about $42 a month in CPU alone. Its WebSockets are also cut off after **60 minutes at most** ([Cloud Run WebSockets](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/triggering/websockets)). UPI payment is documented only for "a qualified organization in India" ([Google Cloud UPI](https://docs.cloud.google.com/billing/docs/resources/upi-payment-india)). AWS ended its 12-month free micro instances for accounts created on or after **15 July 2025**. New accounts now get **$100 plus up to $100 more in credit for 6 months** ([AWS What's New](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2025/07/aws-free-tier-credits-month-free-plan/)), and a Free-plan account **closes** when either runs out ([AWS free tier plans](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/awsaccountbilling/latest/aboutv2/free-tier-plans.html)). Azure gives $200 for 30 days and 12 months of B1s, B2pts v2 and B2ats v2 VMs ([Azure account](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-in/pricing/purchase-options/azure-account)). The Arm B2pts v2 is just **2 vCPU / 1 GiB at a 20% CPU baseline** ([Bpsv2-series](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/virtual-machines/sizes/general-purpose/bpsv2-series)). Indian users who sign up directly for pay-as-you-go don't get the 12-month services at all ([Azure free services](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/pricing/free-services)). **Azure for Students** is the one notable no-card offer: $100 for 12 months for full-time students ([Azure for Students](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-in/free/students/)). Tencent offers only a 3-month Lighthouse trial ([Tencent Free Tier](https://www.tencentcloud.com/act/pro/FreeTier)). IBM's Lite apps sleep after 10 days without development activity ([IBM Cloud Free Tier](https://www.ibm.com/cloud/free)). Alibaba closed its India data centres in July 2024 ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2024/07/01/alibaba_cloud_closes_india_australia/)).

Container platforms have mostly removed or shrunk their free tiers. Hugging Face Spaces' free CPU Basic hardware (**2 vCPU / 16 GB**) was the only free container big enough for speech. Since about July 2026, creating Docker Spaces requires **PRO at $9 a month** ([HF Spaces overview](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/spaces-overview); [HF forum](https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/docker-sdk-now-marked-as-paid-when-creating-a-new-space/177580)). Free-hardware Spaces still sleep after 48 hours idle ([HF hardware docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/spaces-gpus)). They also block outbound traffic except on ports 80, 443 and 8080, which would break ADT Bot's email features. Render Free is permanent and needs no card, but it is **512 MB / 0.1 CPU**. It sleeps after 15 minutes without inbound traffic, has **no persistent disk** and blocks SMTP ports ([Render free docs](https://render.com/docs/free)). Keep-alive pings are common practice there and are not explicitly banned, but they don't fix the disk being wiped on every restart. Northflank's Developer Sandbox is "always-on… no sleeping" ([Northflank pricing](https://northflank.com/pricing)), but it requires a payment method and "should not be used for production" ([Northflank billing](https://northflank.com/docs/v1/application/billing/pricing-on-northflank)). Railway's permanent Free plan gives only **$1 a month** of usage ([Railway pricing](https://railway.com/pricing)), about 10–12 days of a 256 MB container. Koyeb now requires a paid plan for new users ([Koyeb blog](https://koyeb.com/blog/koyeb-is-joining-mistral-ai-to-build-the-future-of-ai-infrastructure)). Fly.io has no free allowance for organizations created after October 2024 ([Fly pricing](https://docs.fly.io/about/pricing)). Codespaces and Colab both ban this kind of hosting by their terms ([GitHub product terms](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-for-additional-products-and-features); [Colab FAQ](https://research.google.com/colaboratory/faq.html)). One free piece is still useful: a Cloudflare Workers Free cron trigger, which can ping a sleeping host ([Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)).

Trial credit can only bridge a gap. Akamai/Linode gives **$100 for 60 days** with a Mumbai region ([Linode](https://www.linode.com/lp/free-credit-100-5000/)). Vultr gives credit "for up to 30 days" ([Vultr docs](https://docs.vultr.com/platform/create-an-account)) and has Mumbai, Bangalore and Delhi NCR regions ([Vultr blog](https://blogs.vultr.com/Vultr-expands-in-India-now-available-in-Bangalore-and-Delhi-NCR)). DigitalOcean's pricing FAQ now describes a **$5** credit ([DigitalOcean pricing](https://www.digitalocean.com/pricing)), where it long advertised $200. One source dates the change to mid-2026, though that date is unconfirmed ([Findstack](https://findstack.com/resources/digitalocean-free-credit)). All of these need a card and start billing automatically if you forget the server.

## A spare PC at home beats a Pi 5 that now costs ₹17,000+

If you already own a 4-core x86 laptop or mini PC with 8 GB or more, it is the best speech host on this list, and its running cost is small. An Intel N100 mini PC measures about **14 W idle and 29 W under load** ([LowEndTalk](https://lowendtalk.com/discussion/comment/3729346/)). At Indian tariffs of roughly ₹6–10 per marginal unit ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/hike-in-fixed-charge-to-raise-power-bill-in-karnataka-3466600); [housiey](https://housiey.com/blogs/?p=10074)), that is on the order of **₹1,000 a year**, against ₹5,900 or more a year for even the cheapest suitable VPS. The local M1 result above (Whisper base at about real time, Kokoro at about twice real time) is the right ballpark for a modern 4-core machine. Remote access is free. Tailscale's Personal plan now covers **6 users** for non-commercial use ([Tailscale pricing](https://tailscale.com/pricing)). Cloudflare Tunnel makes outbound-only connections, so it works without a public IP or port forwarding, but a stable hostname needs your own domain ([flaviocopes](https://flaviocopes.com/cloudflare-tunnel/)). The trade-offs are your home's power cuts and upload speed, which no cloud option shares.

The Raspberry Pi 5 is no longer the cheap choice. RAM-crisis price rises took the **8 GB model from $80 to $175 and the 16 GB to $305** by April 2026 ([BGR](https://www.bgr.com/2150106/raspberry-pi-cost-ram-crisis/)), and the 2 GB rose again on 1 October 2026 ([OMG! Ubuntu](https://www.omgubuntu.co.uk/2026/10/raspberry-pi-2gb-costs-how-much-now/amp)). At 1 USD ≈ ₹95.75 ([conversormoedas.pt](https://conversormoedas.pt/cambio/inr/usd)), the 8 GB is about ₹16,750 before import duty and GST. One Indian listing shows ₹23,749 ([Smartprix](https://www.smartprix.com/raspberry_pi/raspberry_pi-brand/price-below_25000)). The Pi does draw only **3–9 W** ([raspberry.tips](https://raspberry.tips/en/faq/raspberry-pi-power-consumption-update-2026-all-models-compared)), about ₹260–770 a year in electricity. It runs the Node server and Chromium comfortably, but speech is borderline. Stock Kokoro runs at roughly real time; a specially optimised int8 build reaches a real-time factor of 0.37–0.65, and Piper reaches 0.12–0.14 ([kokoro-pi](https://github.com/zreecespieces/kokoro-pi)). Small Whisper models are "best kept to batch jobs" ([promptquorum](https://www.promptquorum.com/power-local-llm/local-whisper-stt-comparison-2026)). A Pi 5 performs about like the Oracle A1 for speech, and the Oracle VM costs nothing.

If a paid VPS turns out to be simpler than home hosting, the cheapest box found that fits everything is **Contabo's Cloud VPS 4 (4 vCPU / 8 GB) at about €4.40–5.50 a month (≈₹490–615), with an India location** ([Contabo](https://contabo.com/en/vps/)). Contabo is known for overselling, so its 4 cores will be slower than dedicated ones. Hetzner's better-value CX33 (€8.99) and CAX21 (€10.99) have shown as **"currently unavailable"** since a hardware shortage began on 26 June 2026 ([Hetzner](https://www.hetzner.com/cloud/cost-optimized/); [pulsetic](https://pulsetic.com/status/hetzner/incidents/3687/)), and Hetzner has no India region. For the Node server alone, OVHcloud's India VPS-1 starts at **₹420 ex-GST** ([OVHcloud India](https://www.ovhcloud.com/en-in/)).

## Conclusion

The pattern behind the 2026 changes matters more than any single offer. Free and cheap general compute is getting scarcer because the AI-driven RAM and hardware squeeze hits exactly the cores and memory a self-hosted speech server needs: Oracle halved its free VM, Hetzner sold out, Pi prices doubled, and the free container platforms are closing. Hosted speech inference is moving the opposite way, with free and near-free Whisper-class transcription arriving from Groq, Cloudflare and Mistral. The durable design for ADT Bot is therefore a light always-on control plane — Node, sync, routines and Chromium — on whatever free or home box is available, with speech treated as a swappable service rather than something the server must run. ADT Bot already supports that through Cloud Whisper and its native TTS providers. With that design, losing any one provider costs a settings change instead of a migration.

Three unknowns deserve a real test before you commit. First, actual faster-whisper-tiny/base and Kokoro timings on a 2-OCPU A1 in Mumbai or Hyderabad. Second, whether an Indian card gets through Oracle signup, and whether A1 capacity is actually available in those single-availability-domain regions. Third, round-trip latency from your own network to Groq and to Azure's Central India speech endpoint. Keep a cheap fallback chain configured from day one: `gpt-4o-mini-transcribe` at about $0.36 a month for speech-to-text, then Google Cloud TTS or Deepgram credit behind Azure F0 for text-to-speech, so a quiet free-tier cut like Oracle's in June doesn't silence the bot.
